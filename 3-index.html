Idghaam: the merging rule

Tajweed lesson · الإدغام

When a quiet noon (نْ) or a tanween ending meets one of six letters, its sound melts into the next letter. This page shows the two kinds of idghaam, with examples from the Quran and a short practice set.

الإِدْغَام

Idghaam means merging. Instead of saying the noon clearly, you let it flow into the letter that follows, so the two sounds become one.

In the examples below, the gold letter is the noon or tanween that disappears, and the olive letter is the one it melts into.

The six letters

Every idghaam letter is in يرملون

Idghaam happens only before the six letters gathered in the word يَرْمَلُون. Those six letters split into two groups, and each group has its own sound.

يyaa رraa مmeem لlaam وwaw نnoon

The first kind

Idghaam with ghunnah · إدغام بغنة

Before ي، ن، م، و (gathered in the word يَنْمُو), the noon melts in and leaves behind a gentle nasal hum called ghunnah. Hold the hum for about two counts.

كِتَابٌ مُّبِينٌ
With ghunnah · tanween melts into meem, keep the hum
Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:15
مَنْ يَعْمَلْ سُوءًا
With ghunnah · noon melts into yaa, keep the hum
Surah An-Nisa 4:123
مِنْ وَرَائِهِ جَهَنَّمُ
With ghunnah · noon melts into waw, keep the hum
Surah Ibrahim 14:16
خَلَقَكُم مِّنْ نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ
With ghunnah · noon melts into noon, keep the hum
Surah An-Nisa 4:1

The second kind

Idghaam without ghunnah · إدغام بغير غنة

Before ل and ر the noon disappears completely into the letter. There is no hum at all, just one clean, merged sound.

أُولَٰئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدًى مِنْ رَّبِّهِمْ
Without ghunnah · noon melts fully into raa, no hum
Surah Al-Baqarah 2:5
فَمَنْ لَّمْ يَجِدْ
Without ghunnah · noon melts fully into laam, no hum
Surah An-Nisa 4:92
A small checkIf you feel a buzz in your nose while merging, you are doing idghaam with ghunnah. Before ل and ر there should be no buzz at all.

Try it yourself

Practice

Say each word out loud, decide the kind of idghaam, then check your answer.

غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ
Which kind of idghaam is this?
Without ghunnah. The tanween melts into raa completely, with no nasal hum.
مِنْ مَّالٍ
Which kind of idghaam is this?
With ghunnah. Meem is one of the يَنْمُو letters, so the noon melts in with a hum.
مَنْ يُؤْمِنُ
Which kind of idghaam is this?
With ghunnah. Yaa takes the noon's sound and keeps the hum for two counts.
مِنْ لَّيْلٍ
Which kind of idghaam is this?
Without ghunnah. Laam pulls the noon in fully, with no hum at all.

Read each example slowly first, then at your normal recitation speed.
